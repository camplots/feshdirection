INSTALLATION INSPECTIONS

DON’T HOPE.
INSIST.

A solar or battery installation should not simply be assumed to be compliant because the system has been switched on.

The Clean Energy Regulator’s latest solar-battery inspection results show that Queensland had the highest share of substandard installations among the states included in the report. Across all inspected battery systems, 62.28% were rated substandard.

62.28%

In the regulator’s terms, substandard means technically non-compliant, although safe to remain in operation.

If you proceed with an installer introduced through Watts Better, we arrange an independent inspection at no additional cost to you — and you receive the report.

WHY THIS MATTERS

A completed installation can look fine from the ground while important details remain unseen.

Independent inspection provides documented assurance that the installation has been:

  • Installed properly
  • Checked for applicable compliance requirements
  • Assessed for safety
  • Reviewed for workmanship
  • Tested for functioning
  • Recorded in a formal report

The purpose is simple:

THE PURPOSE

You should not have to assume the installation is fine. You should have evidence.

WHAT’S INSPECTED

A licensed electrical inspector independently assesses the accessible parts of your solar or battery installation.

01

Panel condition

The panels are visually assessed for:

  • Correct placement
  • Secure mounting
  • Visible installation or shipping damage
  • General condition

Where thermal imaging is used, the report can identify panels operating outside expected thermal ranges.

02

Inverter

The inverter assessment includes:

  • Model
  • Capacity
  • Firmware version
  • Operating status
  • Available monitoring information

Where monitoring data is available, early generation can be compared with expected output for the system.

03

Mounting and structure

The inspection reviews:

  • Mounting rails
  • Clamps
  • Roof penetration points
  • Sealing
  • Structural installation
  • Alignment with manufacturer requirements and applicable standards
04

Wiring and connections

The inspection covers:

  • DC string cables
  • MC4 connectors
  • DC isolators
  • AC isolators
  • Inverter-to-switchboard connections
  • Secure connections
  • Required labelling
05

Generation performance

Where data is available, early generation is compared with expected output for:

  • Rated system capacity
  • Location
  • Orientation
  • Operating conditions

This provides a practical indication of whether the system is functioning as expected.

06

Compliance status

The installation is assessed against the applicable requirements, including relevant Australian Standards and inverter connection requirements.

The report records the areas assessed and provides clear evidence of the inspection outcome.

WHAT YOU RECEIVE

You receive a formal independent report that records:

  • The areas inspected
  • The assessment findings
  • Relevant photographs or photographic evidence
  • Any matters requiring attention
  • The overall inspection outcome

The purpose of the report is to give you a clear record of the completed installation.

INDEPENDENT INSPECTION, NOT INSTALLER APPROVAL

THE INSTALLER REMAINS RESPONSIBLE FOR

  • The site assessment
  • The final system design
  • Equipment selection
  • Installation
  • Certification
  • Network requirements
  • Rectification of installation issues

THE INSPECTION DOES NOT REPLACE

  • The installer’s responsibilities
  • Required electrical certification
  • Network approval
  • State or territory electrical-safety requirements
  • Government inspection or compliance programs

The independent inspection provides a separate assessment of the completed installation.

The Clean Energy Regulator’s inspection program itself is designed to complement, not replace, state and territory electrical-safety programs.
