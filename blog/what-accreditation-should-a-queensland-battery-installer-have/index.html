What accreditation should a Queensland battery installer have?

Solar PV and battery work are not the same accreditation category. Here is what homeowners should check before accepting a quote.

Watts Better · September 2026

A company can advertise solar and batteries without making it obvious who will actually design, install and sign off the work.

That is why it is important to check the individual responsible for the installation — not just the business name or the equipment brand.

For a system claiming Australian Government support, the relevant accreditation and licensing requirements matter.

Solar and battery accreditation are not the same question

Solar PV and battery systems are related, but they are not identical installation categories.

Solar Accreditation Australia has separate accreditation classes for different system types, including:

  • Grid-connected photovoltaic systems
  • Grid-connected battery systems
  • Stand-alone power systems

If a quote includes a battery, ask whether the person responsible for the battery work holds the relevant battery accreditation.

A general solar accreditation should not be assumed to cover every type of battery installation.

  • Source: Solar Accreditation Australia — accreditation

Check the individual installer

Ask for:

  • The accredited person's name
  • Their Solar Accreditation Australia accreditation number
  • The relevant accreditation class
  • Their Queensland electrical licence details
  • Confirmation of who will attend the site
  • Confirmation of who will test and commission the system
  • Confirmation of who will sign off the installation

The company arranging the work and the individual performing or signing off the work may not always be the same.

You should know who is responsible.

The Australian Government recommends checking the installer's accreditation number and status before seeking a quote.

  • Source: Australian Government Solar Guide

Check the products too

The installer's accreditation is only one part of the picture.

Also ask whether:

  • The solar modules are approved
  • The inverter is approved
  • The battery is eligible under the relevant program
  • The exact quoted model matches the model to be installed
  • The product warranty is clear
  • Australian technical and warranty support is available
  • Replacement parts can be obtained
  • The system is suitable for the proposed installation

The brand name on the quote is not enough.

Check the exact model.

What does NETCC approval mean?

The New Energy Tech Consumer Code is a voluntary consumer-protection code for sellers of solar, batteries and other new energy technology.

An approved seller has committed to standards covering areas such as:

  • Sales and marketing
  • Quotes and contracts
  • Installation
  • Safety
  • Warranties
  • Customer support
  • Complaints handling

NETCC approval is useful, but it is not a substitute for checking:

  • The individual installer
  • The electrical licence
  • The battery accreditation
  • The proposed equipment
  • The contract
  • The installation scope

You can search the approved-seller register through NETCC.

Questions to ask before signing

Ask the installer:

  • Who will physically install the system?
  • Who will supervise the work?
  • Who will attend the site during installation?
  • Who will test and commission the system?
  • Who will sign the compliance documentation?
  • Does that person hold the relevant battery accreditation?
  • Is the installer licensed to complete the electrical work in Queensland?
  • Which exact battery model will be installed?
  • Is the product eligible for the claimed discount?
  • What documents will I receive after commissioning?
  • Will you cooperate with an independent post-installation inspection?

A reputable installer should be comfortable answering these questions clearly.

Accreditation does not guarantee perfect work

Accreditation is important.

It does not mean every installation will be perfect.

The Clean Energy Regulator's battery inspection program reports installation issues involving matters such as:

  • Labelling
  • Wiring
  • Protection
  • Backup circuits
  • Mechanical protection
  • Overcurrent protection

The regulator's inspection results also distinguish between adequate, substandard and unsafe installations.

That is why accreditation before installation and independent inspection afterwards are separate protections.

Keep the documents

After installation, keep copies of:

  • Final system design
  • Quote and contract
  • Product details
  • Warranty documents
  • Certificate of electrical safety or compliance
  • Network approval
  • Commissioning information
  • Monitoring access
  • STC documentation
  • Installer accreditation details
  • Any inspection report

Good documentation can matter years later if the system needs servicing, ownership changes or a warranty issue arises.
