The Queensland home battery discount: what changes over time?

The Cheaper Home Batteries Program changes its STC factor every six months. Here is what Brisbane homeowners need to understand before relying on a rebate estimate.

Watts Better · September 2026

The federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program is helping reduce the upfront cost of eligible battery installations across Australia.

For Brisbane homeowners, the program can make a battery more affordable.

But the discount should not be the only reason to install one.

The important questions are:

  • Does your home use enough electricity to make a battery useful?
  • Is there enough solar energy available to charge it?
  • Is the battery the right size?
  • Does the quoted saving reflect your actual tariff?
  • Is the installation being completed by the appropriately accredited people?
  • What discount actually applies when your battery is installed?

The answer depends on more than the rebate headline.

What is the Cheaper Home Batteries Program?

The Cheaper Home Batteries Program provides support for eligible small-scale battery systems through the expansion of the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme.

The support is provided through small-scale technology certificates, commonly called STCs.

The program is available to eligible:

  • Households
  • Small businesses
  • Community organisations

The program supports battery systems from 5 kWh to 100 kWh, subject to the program requirements.

The Australian Government describes the support as a discount of around 30% on the upfront cost for a range of eligible systems.

  • Source: Australian Government — Cheaper Home Batteries Program

You do not usually apply for the discount yourself

Homeowners generally do not submit a separate application to the Australian Government or the Clean Energy Regulator.

The installer or registered agent usually manages the STC process and passes the value through as an upfront discount on the quote or invoice.

That means you should ask the installer to show:

  • The battery system price
  • The estimated discount
  • Any other rebate or incentive
  • The final price after the discount
  • Any fees or exclusions
  • The assumptions used to calculate the discount

Do not rely only on a headline such as “rebate included”.

Ask to see the calculation.

The discount changes over time

The value of the support is connected to the STC factor that applies when the battery is installed.

The current published schedule is:

The discount that applies to your installation is determined by the relevant program settings when the battery is installed — not simply when you first request a quote.

  • Source: DCCEEW — STC factor schedule

Battery size also matters

The program does not treat every kilowatt-hour of battery capacity in exactly the same way.

From 1 May 2026, the support structure was adjusted according to system size.

The program continues to support systems up to 100 kWh, but the level of support tapers across larger capacities.

This means a larger battery does not automatically receive the same support per kilowatt-hour as a smaller battery.

Ask your installer:

  • What is the usable battery capacity?
  • What capacity is being used for the STC calculation?
  • How much of the system receives the highest level of support?
  • What capacity receives a lower level of support?
  • Is the quoted discount based on the exact battery model?
  • Does the quoted discount assume installation before a particular date?
  • What happens if installation is delayed?

The answer should be shown clearly in the proposal.

What does this mean for Brisbane homeowners?

The discount is national, but the value of a battery in Brisbane still depends on your household.

A battery may be more useful when:

  • Your solar system exports a significant amount of energy during the day
  • Your household uses more electricity in the afternoon or evening
  • Your grid import rate is higher than your feed-in tariff
  • Your battery can be regularly charged and discharged
  • Your system is correctly sized
  • You expect to remain at the property long enough to benefit from the system

A battery may be less useful when:

  • Your household uses little electricity
  • Most of your consumption already occurs during daylight
  • Your existing solar system produces little surplus energy
  • The proposed battery is larger than your actual evening demand
  • The financial result depends mainly on optimistic assumptions
  • The installation cost is high relative to the useful energy the battery can deliver

The discount reduces the upfront cost.

It does not guarantee that the battery will pay for itself quickly.

The discount does not decide the system size

A rebate can make a larger battery look more attractive.

That does not necessarily make a larger battery the right choice.

The system should still be assessed against:

  • Your electricity usage
  • Your evening demand
  • Your solar generation
  • Your export pattern
  • Your tariff
  • Your expected battery utilisation
  • Your available roof and installation space
  • Your budget
  • Your future energy plans

The best battery is not necessarily the battery with the largest discount.

It is the battery that can perform useful work in your home.

What must be checked before the discount applies?

The program has eligibility requirements.

Before accepting a quote, confirm that:

  • The battery is an eligible product
  • The relevant products appear on the applicable approved product lists
  • The installer is accredited for the relevant battery work
  • The installer holds the required Queensland electrical licence
  • The system will be installed in accordance with applicable requirements
  • The installation will be complete and commissioned
  • The STC paperwork will identify the responsible installer
  • The discount will be shown clearly in the contract

The Clean Energy Regulator states that designers and installers must be accredited by Solar Accreditation Australia for the relevant system type, and installers must also meet applicable electrical licensing requirements.

  • Source: Clean Energy Regulator — rooftop solar installers and designers

Ask what happens if the installation is delayed

The discount settings depend on the relevant installation period.

Before signing, ask:

  • What installation date has been assumed?
  • What happens if the installation moves into the next STC period?
  • Who carries the difference if the discount changes?
  • Is the quoted price fixed?
  • Is the battery currently available?
  • Is the installer waiting on network approval?
  • Are all required upgrades included?
  • What happens if the final site assessment changes the system?

Get the answer in writing.

A rebate estimate should not be treated as a guaranteed final price unless the contract clearly says what happens if circumstances change.

Queensland programs are not all the same

The federal battery discount should not be confused with other Queensland energy programs.

For example:

  • The Supercharged Solar for Renters program provides eligible Queensland landlords with rebates of up to $3,500 for installing solar on qualifying rental properties. It is a solar-rental program, not a general household battery rebate. Source: Queensland Government — Supercharged Solar for Renters
  • The former Queensland 44-cent Solar Bonus Scheme is not available to new customers. Existing eligible customers may retain the tariff until its scheduled expiry on 1 July 2028, subject to the scheme rules. Source: Queensland Government — Solar Bonus Scheme

Always check current information through official government websites.

Do not rely on an installer's rebate summary without checking the eligibility conditions that apply to your property and installation.

Do not let the discount replace the analysis

The discount can improve the financial case for a battery.

It cannot answer:

  • How much solar your home can use
  • How often the battery will cycle
  • Whether the battery is too large
  • Whether your tariff suits storage
  • Whether your switchboard needs work
  • Whether the proposed system is suitable for your property
  • Whether the installation will be completed properly

That is why the decision should begin with your electricity data.

Before you accept a battery quote

Ask these questions:

  • What discount has been included?
  • Which STC factor has been used?
  • What happens if installation is delayed?
  • Is the figure based on usable capacity?
  • Why is this battery size suitable for my usage?
  • What tariff has been used?
  • How many cycles per year have been assumed?
  • What is the expected usable capacity over time?
  • Is backup included?
  • Does the battery require a switchboard upgrade?
  • Is the system VPP-capable?
  • Who is responsible for the installation and sign-off?
  • Does the installer hold the relevant battery accreditation?
  • What documents will I receive after commissioning?

A clear installer should be comfortable answering these questions.

The bottom line

The Queensland battery opportunity is real, but the discount is only one part of the decision.

The program can reduce the upfront cost of an eligible battery.

The value of that battery still depends on your home.

Your usage pattern, tariff, solar system, battery size, installation price and workmanship all matter.

Do not choose a battery because the discount exists. Choose one because the numbers and the home support it.
