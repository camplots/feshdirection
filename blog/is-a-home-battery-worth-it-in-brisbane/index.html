Is a home battery worth it in Brisbane?

A battery is not automatically the right choice. Your tariff, usage pattern, solar system and evening demand matter more than a national average.

Watts Better · September 2026

A home battery can reduce the amount of electricity you buy from the grid.

But that does not mean every Brisbane home should install one.

The value of a battery depends on what your home does with energy during the day, when it uses power in the evening, what your tariff charges and how much solar would otherwise be exported.

The right question is not:

THE WRONG QUESTION

“How big a battery can I buy?”

It is:

THE RIGHT QUESTION

“How much useful work can a battery do in my home?”

A battery stores timing, not energy demand

Solar panels generate most of their power during daylight hours.

Many households use more electricity:

  • Before work and school
  • In the late afternoon
  • During the evening
  • After the sun has gone down

A battery can store surplus solar during the day and make it available later.

That can reduce grid purchases, but only if the household has enough surplus solar to charge the battery and enough later demand to use the stored energy.

Your tariff matters

A BATTERY BECOMES MORE VALUABLE WHEN

Grid electricity is expensive during your main usage period. Your feed-in tariff is relatively low. Your solar system exports energy during the day. Your household uses significant electricity in the evening. The battery is used regularly without being oversized.

A BATTERY CAN BE LESS ATTRACTIVE WHEN

Your household uses little electricity. Most of your usage already occurs during daylight. Your solar system produces little surplus energy. Your evening demand is low. The battery is much larger than your typical usable demand.

Bigger is not automatically better

A larger battery costs more.

It also needs enough solar generation and household demand to be used effectively.

An oversized battery may spend much of its time partially used or empty. That can produce a weaker financial result than a smaller system matched to the household.

The battery should be sized around the pattern of your home, not simply around the maximum product available.

What about the Queensland discount?

The federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program provides support through the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme.

The STC factor changes over time. Under the current published schedule, the factor moves from 6.8 during May–December 2026 to 5.7 during January–June 2027, then continues to decline in later periods.

The discount is determined by the applicable settings when the battery is installed, and eligibility depends on the program rules, approved products and accredited installation requirements.

  • Source: DCCEEW

That makes timing relevant, but timing should not replace proper analysis.

A rebate does not turn an unsuitable battery into a good investment.

What should you do first?

Before asking an installer for a battery price, understand:

  • Your average energy use
  • Your evening usage
  • Your tariff periods
  • Your export pattern
  • Your current solar position
  • The battery size that your home could realistically use
